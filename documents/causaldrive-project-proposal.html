CausalDrive · Project proposal01
01 · Scope

Research question and system features

Research question: How can multimodal world models learn efficient, action-controllable and uncertainty-aware representations that support real-time decisions—and which principles transfer between autonomous driving and other embodied agents?

1Multimodal pretraining

Jointly model video, agent actions, physical state and optional language/task context using self-supervised objectives.

2Structured latent state

A compact visual representation augmented with ego motion, drivable-area, lane and dynamic-object features.

3Action-conditioned rollout

A small recurrent or transformer dynamics model predicts 0.5–2 seconds ahead under a proposed action sequence.

4Multiple futures

Probabilistic or ensemble prediction represents ambiguity rather than collapsing every situation into one average future.

5Action fidelity

Counterfactual tests verify that steering and speed actually change predictions in physically and geometrically consistent ways.

6Uncertainty

Calibration and out-of-distribution tests identify when rollout predictions should not be trusted by a planner.

7Scalable ML systems

Multi-GPU training, data streaming, mixed precision and profiling connect local research to foundation-model engineering.

8Deployment and transfer

C++/CUDA inference supports CARLA closed loop, followed by one small navigation or manipulation transfer study.

Explicit non-goals

The project will not attempt frontier-scale pretraining, an entire driving stack, or several full robotics projects. Scaling is studied through controlled experiments; a second domain remains a small transfer test rather than a competing project.

Primary hypothesis

Action-consistency objectives, planning-aware latents and dynamic token routing can improve controllability and task utility per unit of compute; the resulting principles should transfer beyond the original driving domain.

02 · Research

Experiments and potential contributions

A. Action-fidelity benchmark

Hold the initial scene fixed while varying steering and speed. Compare recorded, zeroed, reversed and shuffled actions. Measure whether predicted ego motion, lanes, objects and optical flow respond correctly.

Most feasible paperLow–medium compute

B. Planning-aware representation

Compare reconstruction-oriented latents against structured states trained for ego motion, occupancy and risk. Test whether visual quality predicts downstream planning performance.

Strong research questionMedium compute

C. Calibrated multiple futures

Compare deterministic, ensemble and probabilistic rollouts. Evaluate calibration, diversity, risk coverage and behaviour under weather, lighting and action distribution shifts.

Research extensionMedium compute

D. Scalable, action-aware efficiency

Allocate tokens by action and horizon; measure quality, throughput and memory from one GPU to multi-GPU training, then validate the method in a second embodied task.

05 · Outcomes

Success criteria, outputs and risks

Research success

  • A precise contribution beyond model implementation.
  • Action-conditioned gains against an identical action-free baseline.
  • Counterfactual evaluation and one cross-domain transfer study.
  • Ablations, negative results and statistical uncertainty.
  • A first-author paper, workshop submission or public preprint.

Engineering success

  • Reproducible training from a documented data subset.
  • Tested Python, C++ and GPU-optimised inference paths.
  • Measured batch-one latency, memory and multi-GPU scaling.
  • Resumable distributed runs, Docker, CI and versioned experiments.
  • Short interactive or recorded closed-loop demonstration.

Principal risks and mitigations

RiskMitigation
Model ignores the action signal.Action-free control model, shuffled actions, counterfactual sweeps and explicit action-consistency loss.
Visual generation consumes the compute budget.Make structured latent prediction primary; keep decoding diagnostic and low resolution.
Observational data cannot validate interventions.Use CARLA for controlled counterfactual tests and clearly separate real-data from simulator claims.
Scaling becomes performative rather than scientific.Report throughput, memory and convergence from controlled 1–4 GPU tests; make no fleet-scale claims.
Project expands indefinitely.Freeze the driving MVP at month 12; keep the second domain to one transfer experiment.
Solo research lacks feedback or references.Seek periodic academic review; retain project leadership and first authorship while collaborating where valuable.
